# Strategy 5: RSI(2) short-term mean reversion

- Family: mean reversion (short-term, daily)
- Source: Larry Connors and Cesar Alvarez, *Short Term Trading Strategies That Work* (2008).
  Citation from general knowledge; check the details against the book.
- Code: [`library/rsi2.py`](../../strategies/src/tp_strategies/library/rsi2.py)
  (`tp-backtest run rsi2`)
- Stage: 1 (backtest)

## Economic rationale

Inside a long-term uptrend, a sharp two- or three-day selloff is more often an overreaction than
news that changes the trend: liquidity demanders (forced sellers, stop-loss cascades, headline
traders) push the price below where patient buyers will take the other side, and it snaps back
within days. The 200-day filter keeps the strategy out of names in real decline, where dips
keep dipping.

Who is on the other side: short-term sellers paying for immediacy. The strategy is a liquidity
provider over a few days, paid for absorbing their selling.

## Rules

| | |
|---|---|
| Universe | SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA and the 38 large caps |
| Trend filter | Close above the 200-day simple moving average |
| Entry | 2-period RSI (Wilder smoothing) closes below 10; the most oversold first |
| Exit | Close above the 5-day simple moving average, or a 10-session time stop |
| Sizing | Up to 10 positions at 10% of the sleeve each; at most 3 per GICS sector |
| Stops | None besides the time stop (the source found price stops hurt this kind of system) |
| Execution | Entries and exits at the next session's open, 5 bps slippage |

Keep `1/slots` at or under 10% while single stocks are in the universe (the position cap); an
ETF-only universe can use fewer, larger slots.

## What the source reports (to check against)

Headline claims: stocks and index ETFs above their 200-day average that closed with a very low
2-period RSI had above-average returns over the following days, more so the lower the RSI, and
exiting on a close above the 5-day average captured most of the bounce. Adding stop-losses
reduced performance.

## Known failure regimes

- **Trend breaks**: a dip that is the start of a bear market (Oct 2008, Feb 2020) is bought, and
  the time stop exits after most of the damage.
- **Gaps against open positions**: earnings or news gaps overnight on single stocks; no stop
  protects against them.
- **Low-volatility grinds**: few entry signals and the strategy sits in cash.
- **Interaction with the daily loss limit**: the risk service blocks new risk after a 2% down
  day for the book, which is exactly when this strategy wants to buy. It will miss some entries
  by design.
- **Costs**: daily turnover makes slippage the biggest drag; the backtest's 5 bps per fill must
  be confirmed in paper trading.
